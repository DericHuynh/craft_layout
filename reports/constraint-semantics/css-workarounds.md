# What happens when a Craft author skips the CSS workaround?

We took published CSS recipes, wrote down the component geometry we wanted, and tried the direct declaration first. The trials changed the viewport, content, item count or footer size on the same tree. The controls included explicit hard minima, visible and clipped content, an empty grid slot and an out-of-flow overlay. These details distinguish a layout that meets the request from one that merely looks right at one size.

The [follow-up edge cases](edge-cases.md) cover grid minima, sticky limits, wrapping and writing direction, including a replay in Chrome 158.

The [cross-engine follow-up](author-intent.md) measures the original repairs against native Craft, Taffy, Yoga, Chromium and Godot.

## Public Rust API

| Component and original recipe | What the author asked for | What Craft did |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Long toolbar title](https://defensivecss.dev/tip/flexbox-min-content-size/) | Keep a 40px action and an 8px gap. Clip a 288px measured title inside its share. | At toolbar widths 224 and 144, a plain `Fill` slot got 176 and 96. Explicit zero changed nothing. A genuine 160px slot minimum pushed the action's right edge to 208 in the 144px toolbar. |
| [Nested scrolling list](https://defensivecss.dev/tip/flexbox-min-content-size/) | Keep a 36px header in view and scroll eight 30px rows below it. | At heights 160 and 112, the pane got 124 and 76. Its scroll range ended at 116 and 164 respectively. Setting the wrapper's minimum height to zero gave the same rectangles. |
| [Equal cards with unequal padding](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/32099624/flexbox-item-widths-are-affected-by-padding) | Give three cards equal outer widths, with 18px side padding on the middle one. | In 300px, three `Fill` siblings measured 100 each and the middle content measured 64. At 240px they measured 80 each and the middle content measured 44. Equal flex *growth* from different preferred widths yielded 64, 140 and 96 in the 300px control. |
| [Sidebar beside a carousel](https://defensivecss.dev/tip/grid-min-content-size/) | Keep a 72px sidebar and 12px gap visible. Scroll 360px of cards locally. | At widths 320 and 260, `Track::Fr(1.)` gave the main column 236 and 176. The carousel scrolled by 124 and 184 and the outer grid had zero scroll range. An explicit zero floor gave the same result. A protected 360px content floor moved that overflow to the outer grid. |
| [Responsive card grid](https://smolcss.dev/#smol-responsive-css-grid) | Wrap near 120px with a 10px gap and fit one card into a narrower panel. | The first author's `AutoFit { minimum: 120., .. }` kept the card 120px wide at 90px. The added `TrackAxis::auto_fit_within(120.)` produced three 120px tracks at 380, two 184.5px tracks at 379, one 249px track at 249, and one 90px track at 90. An explicit 120px floor still produces 30px of overflow. |
| [Sparse grid](https://defensivecss.dev/tip/auto-fit-fill/) | Either expand the remaining cards or keep their original slots when a filter removes items. | In 380px, fitting one, two and three cards gave widths 380, 185 and 120. Reserving empty slots kept cards at 120 in every case. Both outcomes follow a user-visible design choice. |
| [Alternating chart and help panel](https://www.hsablonniere.com/prevent-layout-shifts-with-css-grid-stacks--qcj5jo/) | Reserve the height of the larger view while they overlap. | A free-layout group with fitted height reserved 70px for 42px and 70px children, then 90px when the chart grew. Marking the 70px view as absolutely positioned reduced the initial reservation to 42px. Showing or hiding the inactive view is a renderer decision. |
| [Clearfix and overflow containment](https://rachelandrew.co.uk/archives/2017/01/24/the-end-of-the-clearfix-hack/) | Enclose a 64px float and leave a 20px status badge visible across the card edge. | An isolated block enclosed the float and exposed all 20px of the badge at two card widths. Overflow-hidden enclosed the float but exposed only 8px. A clearing child also worked. The ordinary block was 16px tall. The field documentation now describes float enclosure. |
| [One pixel of padding to stop a collapsing margin](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2019/07/margins-in-css/) | Keep a heading 12px inside its card. | Block isolation gave a 12px inset and a 32px card. The 1px padding recipe gave 13px and 33px. Ordinary block flow moved the entire card down by 12px. |
| [Footer at the bottom of a short page](https://philipwalton.github.io/solved-by-flexbox/demos/sticky-footer/) | Let the middle expand on a short story and put a variable-height footer after a long one. | Growing the footer from 30 to 44 in a 120px viewport moved its short-page top from 90 to 76. On the long page its top stayed at 195. A [fixed height reservation](https://codepen.io/chriscoyier/pen/jqRXBz/) left the larger footer ending at 134 on the short page. |
| [Center an unknown-size dialog](https://www.w3.org/Style/Examples/007/center.en.html) | Keep the dialog centered as the content and viewport change. | `Pos::Center` put an 80×32 dialog at (120,74) in 320×180 and a 120×48 dialog at (60,48) in 240×144. Reusing fixed half-size offsets landed the edited dialog at (80,56). The published percentage-translation recipe computes the element's half-size dynamically. |
| [Responsive video ratio](https://alistapart.com/article/creating-intrinsic-ratios-for-video/) | Give a half-width video its own 16:9 height. | `Size::AspectRatio(16. / 9.)` gave 160×90 and 240×135. The original percentage-padding technique also worked with a half-width containing wrapper. Removing that wrapper made the padding follow the full parent width and doubled the height to 180 and 270. |

An independent reviewer tried the new constructor from a separate Rust client, using the public documentation. Four cards shared a padded pane beside a rail. With a 73px target and a 7px gap, a 233px pane fit three 73px columns. At 232px it fit two 112.5px columns. In a 54px pane the flexible card used 54px and the hard-floor control used 73px. All 32 predicted card rectangles matched over eight solves with zero warnings or rule issues. The reviewer made no layout edits after the first run and suggested clarifying the meaning of the target width in the README.

## Importing literal CSS

The same intent can arrive as low-level CSS longhands through the fixture importer. We exercised that path independently of the native API. A local Qt WebEngine 5.15.2 build, Chromium 83.0.4103.122, supplied the browser measurements for all 15 checked-in CSS probes. Thirteen had matching node rectangles. Two differences are recorded below. We used an 8px margin for the toolbar comparison so that flex-gap support did not affect it. The scroller comparison used a zero-width scrollbar. This is a versioned reference for those exact inputs.

| Imported component | Craft | Browser control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 224px toolbar, 288px rigid descendant and 40px action | Automatic minimum gave a 288px title slot, action at x=296. With `min-width: 0` the slot was 176px and the action began at 184. | Same result in both cases. A native `Fill` slot takes the available width directly. |
| 160px column with a non-scrolling wrapper around a scrolling list | Wrapper and inner viewport were 124px tall with and without `min-height: 0`. Scroll extent was 240px. | Without the reset the wrapper was 240px tall and the list had no internal scrolling. Adding it gave the intended 124px viewport. |
| Three equally flexible, content-box cards with 36px of padding on the middle card | Outer widths were 100/100/100 with padding on the card or inside it. | Outer padding produced 88/124/88. Moving padding to an inner wrapper produced 100/100/100. |
| [A 100%-height grandchild inside a growing flex item](https://github.com/philipwalton/flexbugs/issues/197) | A 100px flex column and 20px header left 80px. The growing item and its grandchild both measured 80px with auto basis, zero basis or zero height on the item. | Same geometry in all three cases. The column had a definite height. |
| `1fr 40px` grid in a 240px panel with a 320px-wide grandchild | Bare CSS `1fr` protected 320px and the rail started at 320. With `minmax(0, 1fr)` the rail started at 200. In both cases the grandchild measured 320px. | Same track widths and grandchild geometry. Shrinking the track alone still allows the grandchild to extend into the rail. |
| Responsive grid at 90px | The importer now accepts `repeat(auto-fit, minmax(min(100%, 120px), 1fr))` and the reversed order of the two `min()` arguments. The track is 90px in both cases. A hard `minmax(120px, 1fr)` track is 120px. | Both argument orders measured 90px, the hard minimum measured 120px, and a 250px control had two 120px tracks with the third card at (0,20). |

The scroller and the padded cards are concrete CSS compatibility differences beyond the pinned Taffy corpus. Their Craft results satisfy these authors' requests. A project porting CSS that relies on the original browser geometry can see different results. The new native minimum adds a public `TrackMin::CappedPercent` enum variant, which downstream exhaustive matches must handle. The grid trial exposed missing syntax for unitless zero in a `minmax()` bound as well as the missing responsive minimum. Both the hard-floor overflow and the visible oversized descendant follow specified constraints. We checked rectangles, scroll ranges, warnings and ancestor clips. Browser pixel rendering and Rust memory safety require different tests.

## Reproduce

```sh
cargo test --locked --test workaround_flex --test workaround_flow --test workaround_grid --test workaround_position
cargo test --locked --test conformance -- --workarounds
```

The second command uses the checked-in XML and requires no upstream fixture download. Its expected values are the documented Craft geometry.
